## Reviewer A

## Issues

1. **The non-negotiable is not merely unenforced in this plan — it is absent from it.** PHASE-1.md never restates the purity rule, assigns it to no increment, and gives it no test. Nothing in the module structure enforces it either: the Electron side is one undifferentiated "compositing, export via WebCodecs" lump with no boundary between a pure transform (`frameAt(project, events, t) → frame description`) and the impure shell (decode, canvas, encode). The first concrete violation enters in **increment 4**, which ports "the WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness." That harness's natural loop is decode-driven: for each decoded display frame at PTS τ, consume cursor events up to τ, draw, encode. Under that loop, display-frame selection, cursor position, and PiP content at output time t are emergent properties of VFR capture timing and event-consumption order — not evaluations of a function at tick t. The fixed-step rule demands the inverted loop: for each output tick t_n, sample display at t_n, cursor at t_n, camera at t_n. The condition that exposes the difference is any capture irregularity — a thermal-drop hole (already measured: 18.7 → 12.1 fps), a segment boundary, a display-reconfig gap — because decode-driven and tick-driven semantics pick different source frames and different cursor positions at exactly those points. **The phase boundaries hide it.** Increments 1–3 contain no compositor, so no violation is visible; increment 4's acceptance criterion ("one CFR MP4 with cursor overlay and camera PiP") is passed by the impure loop; increment 5's soak exercises capture robustness, not transform determinism; and phase 1 ships no preview, so no second driver exists to diff against. The divergence surfaces only when preview is built later — necessarily against t — at which point export is the entrenched reference implementation and the mismatch will be diagnosed as a preview bug. That is the exact bug class, with the polarity reversed. Note the helper side is actually the right substrate: events.json, anchors.json, and logged world-change events are precisely the inputs a pure transform needs. The failure is that nothing on the Electron side is required to consume them as a function of t.

2. **Export's input model is undefined: segmented capture vs. "one CFR MP4."** The risk table mandates segmenting output every N minutes; the architecture diagram shows `display.mp4` singular; increment 4 promises one CFR MP4 and never mentions stitching. Segment-boundary timestamp continuity, hold-last-across-segment-gap, and per-segment anchors are unplanned work that will be discovered mid-build, and every segment cut that doesn't land on an output tick shifts the t→frame mapping — another place decode-driven stitching and tick-driven sampling diverge. Condition: any recording longer than N minutes, i.e., the soak itself.

3. **The TCC story contradicts itself, and the verification probe tests the wrong variable.** The architecture claims the helper "inherits Electron's TCC identity — one grant." The signing section treats the *helper's* signature stability as what preserves grants — a conclusion carried over from phase 0's standalone one-shot, where the helper was the responsible process. Both cannot be the load-bearing fact. The proposed probe (build, grant, rebuild helper, re-preflight) varies only the helper's signature. If TCC attributes the grant to the Electron parent, the probe's result is unrelated to what it claims to measure — and the self-signed-cert work is either unnecessary (dev Electron's binary signature is already stable across rebuilds of your code) or insufficient. What breaks: either wasted effort minting certificates, or grants that still revoke because the wrong binary was stabilized. Condition: every rebuild in the dev loop.

4. **The stats channel can stall the capture it exists to observe.** JSON-lines over stdout with no stated backpressure policy: if the Electron renderer is busy and stops draining, the pipe fills and the helper's write blocks; if that write shares a thread or queue with capture callbacks, frames drop. What breaks: long-duration stability under exactly the loaded conditions the stats are meant to expose. Condition: any UI stall during recording — opening a session dir, devtools, a GC pause. The fix is a decision the document doesn't make: bounded buffer, drop-oldest on stats, writes off the capture thread.

5. **No acceptance criteria anywhere, including the soak.** "Observable, not inferred" is the right instinct, but there is no drop budget, no fps floor, no pass/fail for the 30-minute soak. Thermal fade at 4K is already measured, so the soak *will* drop frames; whether that fails the slice is undefined, which makes the soak a vibes check. Condition: the first soak run.

6. **Inter-track drift over duration is unmeasured and uninstrumented.** Phase 0 verified CGEvent drift (0.0000 ms) and camera↔mic offset (+1.8 ms median) — but offset is not drift, and the stats list (frames, drops, queue depth, encoder fps) contains no per-track PTS-vs-host-clock drift. What breaks: 30-minute recordings develop lip-sync and PiP-sync skew that nobody sees until export. Condition: the soak's exact scenario — USB camera plus mic over long duration.

## Missing

- **Hole policies, which are transform semantics.** What does the frame at t show during (a) a thermal-drop gap in the display track, (b) a display-reconfig stream rebuild, (c) a camera track dropped after device loss? "Emit an event" supplies the input; the rule for rendering it is defined nowhere, and it must be defined once, in the transform, or export and preview will each invent their own.
- **Where VFR→CFR resampling lives, and its policy** (hold-last? tolerance window?). This is the single most purity-relevant decision in the pipeline and no increment owns it.
- **Alignment ownership.** Which increment consumes anchors.json, with what algorithm — constant offsets from the settled table, or per-anchor correction — and how anchors are emitted per segment.
- **Export resource strategy for 30-minute 4K:** streaming decode vs. whole-file, renderer memory ceiling, expected export wall-time. A slice that OOMs the renderer on the soak recording is not end-to-end.
- **A determinism test — the only cheap enforcement the rule will ever get.** Export twice from the same session dir and pixel-diff the pre-encode frames (byte-diff is too strong if the encoder is nondeterministic). This is the difference between asserting purity and enforcing it, and it fits in one sitting.
- **Fallback if the self-signed cert fails the grant-survival probe**, plus a probe design that also varies the Electron-side identity — or at minimum states which process TCC is expected to attribute.
- **Backpressure and drop policy for the JSON-lines protocol.**

## Overconfidence

- "Composite + export — WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness" reads as near-reuse. The harness is a 12-second one-shot over a single file; its driver loop, memory model, and iteration order are exactly what must change for segmented, long-duration, tick-driven export. This is a rewrite wearing a port's clothing, and its iteration model is the purity risk.
- "Camera/mic are already off the critical path" — true for capture, false for the transform: the PiP puts the camera track on the export critical path, and a camera hole must be rendered by rule, not by absence of data.
- "Inherits Electron's TCC identity — one grant" is stated as settled while the signing section is built on the opposite assumption. Neither is verified on this machine.
- The settled table establishes 4K60 *instantaneous* capability; the quoted thermal fade (18.7 → 12.1 fps) is evidence *against* sustained 4K60, yet the soak implicitly assumes it.
- `capturesAudio` being "present in the 13.3 SDK" says nothing about system audio surviving an SCStream rebuild after display reconfiguration. That path is assumed, not measured.
- The self-signed cert "gives a stable identity so TCC grants survive rebuilds" is stated as fact in the table and admitted as unmeasured two paragraphs later. Keep the admission; strike the table's certainty.

## Verdict

Revise. The capture half of this plan is well-grounded — measured numbers, an honest scope note, risks made observable rather than assumed away. But the document never operationalizes the one rule the architecture exists for: no module boundary names the pure transform, no increment owns it, no test can fail when it is violated, and increment 4 is scheduled to import the exact loop shape — decode-driven, one-shot, harness-born — that violates it structurally while passing every acceptance check the plan defines. The signing, segmentation, and soak-criteria gaps are fixable in passing; this one compounds, because the violating code becomes the reference implementation preview will be judged against. Highest-leverage change: split increment 4 — before any encoder is attached, land `frameAt(project, events, t)` as a pure module with decode and encode behind injected interfaces, drive export from a fixed-step loop over output ticks, and gate it on a render-twice pixel-diff test; then port the spike harness's encode path as a sink behind that interface.

## Reviewer B

## Issues

1. **The pure transform is merely asserted; the module structure hides violations rather than preventing them.**
   *What breaks:* The document states the core non-negotiable is evaluating a pure function $f(\text{project}, \text{events}, t) \to \text{frame}$ at discrete simulation steps $\Delta t$, yet Increment 4 builds export *directly* from WebCodecs without defining a shared evaluator. 
   *Where the violation enters:* The first concrete violation will enter in Increment 4 during cursor smoothing and camera sync. Because `events.json` contains raw Mach timestamped points and video contains VFR presentation timestamps, the exporter must reconcile discrete step $t_k$ to arbitrary event/frame times. Without a standalone, headless reducer that computes `State(t)` from `(events, anchors)`, the author will write stateful decode loops directly inside the WebCodecs export worker (e.g., lerping cursor coordinates based on the previous decoded video frame rather than fixed $t$). 
   *Why phase boundaries hide it:* Phase 1 omits the preview player entirely (listed under Non-Goals / Increment 3 only has "live stats"). Because there is no preview to compare against export, any temporal drift, stateful lerp bugs, or frame-rounding errors inside the export pipeline will produce a seemingly valid MP4. The violation will remain completely invisible until Phase 2 introduces a canvas/DOM preview player, at which point the export pipeline will have to be rewritten.

2. **The TCC inheritance model is incorrect for child helper processes.**
   *What breaks:* Spawning a helper binary via Electron (`child_process.spawn`) does *not* automatically inherit the parent app bundle's TCC identity for `CGEventTapCreate` (Input Monitoring) and `SCStream` (Screen Recording). Under macOS Hardened Runtime, the kernel and TCC daemon inspect the leaf executable's code signature and designated requirement (DR).
   *Under what condition:* A bare Mach-O binary in `Contents/MacOS/` or `Resources/` that lacks its own Info.plist, bundle identifier, and matched entitlements will prompt independently or fail silently at `CGEventTapCreate`, even if the Electron parent was granted permissions by the user.

3. **Segmentation creates PTS discontinuities that break deterministic $t \to \text{sample}$ resolution.**
   *What breaks:* Segmenting MP4 output every $N$ minutes resets or offsets the container timescale and presentation timestamps across files (`display_001.mp4`, `display_002.mp4`). 
   *Under what condition:* When the evaluator requests the screen frame at $t = 301.000\text{s}$, the playback/compositing pipeline must resolve which segment contains $t$, normalize the segment-local PTS back to the global Mach clock, and seek without codec decode delay. Without an explicit manifest schema mapping global mach intervals to segment file offsets and PTS baselines, the evaluation function ceases to be a pure lookup and becomes a stateful file-stitching pipeline.

4. **Stream reconfiguration on display change creates unhandled dimension discontinuities in CFR export.**
   *What breaks:* When `CGDisplayRegisterReconfigurationCallback` fires and the helper rebuilds the `SCStream` at a new display resolution, the captured track changes frame dimensions mid-recording.
   *Under what condition:* Increment 4 specifies a single WebCodecs CFR export pipeline. `VideoEncoder` cannot accept changing frame dimensions without re-initialization (which forces a keyframe and changes container track metadata). The compositor will fail or stretch output frames unless an explicit coordinate-space mapping step normalizes all dynamic source buffers onto a fixed-size canvas.

## Missing

1. **The Evaluation Engine Contract (The Core Rule).**
   The architecture lacks the formal interface definition for the pure evaluator. To satisfy the pure function requirement, the contract must be explicit:
   $$\text{FrameState} = \text{evaluate}(\text{TimelineManifest}, t)$$
   $$\text{render}(\text{FrameState}, \text{SourceFrameProvider}, \text{TargetCanvas})$$
   This separation must exist as an independent package/module that neither knows about WebCodecs export nor DOM preview.

2. **The Frame Index / Anchor Schema (`anchors.json`).**
   The document mentions `anchors.json` but does not specify how it indexes non-deterministic capture streams. It must define the record schema mapping Mach clock intervals $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ to specific PTS offsets, file segment IDs, audio sample offsets, and display scale factors.

3. **Helper Packaging and Entitlements Structure.**
   Missing the definition of the helper's Info.plist, its `CFBundleIdentifier`, and how it is embedded within the Electron app wrapper to allow single-grant TCC inheritance via Apple's App Group / Parent-Child code signing rules.

---

## Overconfidence

1. **"Dev loop: self-signed cert gives a stable identity so TCC grants survive rebuilds."**
   Self-signed certificates in the login keychain only provide a stable Designated Requirement if the Code Directory hash (CDHash) is not the sole constraint. On macOS 13+, rebuilds that alter the Mach-O binary structure often invalidate local TCC authorizations for Input Monitoring (`CGEventTap`) even with a self-signed identity, unless a stable `designated => identifier "..." and anchor ...` requirement string is explicitly passed to `codesign`.

2. **"Drop the affected track, keep recording... Camera/mic are already off the critical path."**
   Handling dynamic device loss in AVFoundation without locking the capture thread is deceptively complex. An unplugged audio device often blocks `AVCaptureAudioDataOutput` dispatch queues or wedges CoreAudio HAL buffers before `AVCaptureSessionRuntimeError` is dispatched, stalling the entire helper runloop.

---

## Verdict

**Revise it.** The capture constraints and hardware realities from Phase 0 are well-analyzed, but the execution structure fails to enforce the author's primary requirement: deterministic preview/export parity. Phase 1 currently builds an isolated capture tool and an isolated WebCodecs exporter; because it defers the preview player, it guarantees that export-specific assumptions will leak into the simulation step, making Phase 1 pass while hiding the architectural violation.

**Highest-leverage change:** Extract the simulation step and compositing math into a zero-dependency pure module `evaluate(timeline, t)`, and require Increment 4 to run identical render passes through both the WebCodecs offline exporter and a bare 1-fps `<canvas>` frame-verification harness before declaring the slice complete.

## Reviewer C

## Issues

1. **The stated purity invariant is not represented in the module boundary at all.** The architecture has only `Electron (UI, compositing, export)` and `Swift helper (capture)`, connected by live JSON-lines plus a session directory. There is no declared `Project`, no canonical event schema, no simulation clock, no replay/transform module, and no rule preventing the compositor from reading current display state, media PTS, decode arrival order, `performance.now()`, or live helper stats. Consequently, “pure function of project + events + t” is an aspiration, not an enforced property.

   The architectural rule should be explicit: **capture produces immutable evidence; replay consumes only an immutable project snapshot, ordered events, and an integer simulation tick. Rendering/export may not query live capture state, wall time, device/display APIs, or decoder arrival timing.**

2. **The first concrete purity violation enters in increment 2, when capture writes independently timed media segments and `events.json` without a defined canonical timeline contract.** `display.mp4`, `camera.mp4`, `mic.wav`, `sysaudio.wav`, `events.json`, and `anchors.json` are listed as sibling outputs, but the document does not define:
   - the recording epoch;
   - whether every timestamp is converted to one canonical unit and origin before persistence;
   - how segment-local PTS maps to that origin;
   - what an SCStream rebuild does to the timeline;
   - how dropped frames, discontinuities, and track loss are represented;
   - what `anchors.json` contains or whether it is authoritative.

   At phase 4, the practical response will be to make the compositor infer alignment from MP4 PTS, segment ordering, current dimensions, and incidental capture metadata. That makes output a function of capture implementation artifacts, not just project + events + `t`. A segment boundary or display reconfiguration is enough to make preview and export choose differently.

   The proposed phase boundaries **hide** this violation. Increment 2 can produce valid-looking files; increment 3 can display live stats; increment 4 can produce a plausible one-off export. Nothing requires two independent replays of the same frozen session to produce identical frame decisions and identical output timing.

3. **“Fixed simulation step” is absent from the actual export plan.** “CFR at export” is not a fixed simulation model. CFR only specifies output frame timestamps. The document does not define:
   - simulation rate;
   - tick representation and rounding rule;
   - mapping from export frame `n` to simulation tick/time;
   - event inclusion rule at a tick boundary;
   - source-frame selection rule for VFR display capture;
   - behavior across source gaps and repeated/missing frames;
   - whether interpolation is prohibited.

   Without these, WebCodecs will likely become the timing authority because it exposes decoded frames asynchronously. That is exactly the preview/export divergence class the design claims to prevent.

4. **Display hot-swap invalidates positional events unless display configuration is part of the immutable replay input.** “Emit an event and rebuild the SCStream against the new config” is insufficient. Cursor coordinates, screen dimensions, display origin, scale factor, capture crop, and display identity can all change. A cursor event at `(x, y)` has no replayable meaning without the display-space transform active at that event’s timestamp. If phase 4 derives this from the current display or the dimensions of whichever segment happens to be decoded, export is non-deterministic and can differ from preview.

5. **The session directory is an unversioned, mutable protocol rather than a project artifact.** The export pipeline is expected to consume files while a long-running helper is segmenting, rebuilding streams, and recording warnings. There is no manifest, finalization marker, atomic segment publication rule, schema version, or immutable session snapshot. Exporting an in-progress session can observe a different file set or metadata state than a later export of “the same” recording. This is both a determinism failure and a recoverability failure after Electron/helper interruption.

6. **The hot-swap and device-loss plans define survival, not output behavior.** “Rebuild the SCStream” and “drop the affected track” leave open whether replay shows a freeze frame, black, transparent PiP, slate, silence, or a discontinuity marker; whether it preserves source time or compresses the gap; and what happens if capture resumes. Those choices must be encoded in project/event data and applied by the transform. Otherwise preview and export will each invent their own behavior.

7. **Increment 2 is not a vertical-slice-sized increment.** It combines four capture paths, event tap, segmented writing, live stats, display reconfiguration, camera/mic loss recovery, and system audio. That is not realistically finishable in one sitting, and it postpones the phase’s central correctness proof—the replay contract—until after most platform integration is built. The result is likely to be a large capture subsystem whose persisted semantics are discovered only when compositing begins.

8. **The proposed soak harness does not test the phase’s stated non-negotiable.** A 30-minute recording with induced failures tests capture survival. It does not test deterministic replay. It needs at minimum: freeze one finalized project/session, render it twice in separate processes, compare per-tick composition decisions and output frame timestamps, and run the same fixture through both preview and export paths. Otherwise it can pass while the primary bug class remains intact.

## Missing

- A versioned, immutable `Project` schema. It should reference content-addressed or finalized media segments, the canonical recording timeline, display configuration intervals, camera layout data, source-track availability intervals, and all transform settings.
- A precise event schema: canonical timestamp type, sequence number/tie-breaker, coordinate space, display ID/configuration revision, and behavior for co-timestamped events.
- A single time model. Define one integer canonical unit—nanoseconds is available already—then define `tick = floor((timeNs - projectStartNs) * simHz / 1e9)` or equivalent, including exact boundary semantics.
- A declared simulation rate and export mapping. For example, whether 60 fps export samples ticks 0, 1, 2… directly, and how a different export FPS maps to fixed simulation steps.
- The transform API and forbidden dependencies. Something equivalent to `frame = transform(project, orderedEvents, tick)` should be independently callable by preview and export. Decode/source selection can be a separate deterministic resolver, but it must take only project data and tick/time as input.
- A deterministic VFR-to-simulation rule: latest frame at or before tick time, nearest frame, hold-last-frame, or another stated policy; plus explicit behavior before the first frame, after the last frame, and across discontinuities.
- A segment manifest with global start/end times, local PTS mapping, capture configuration revision, dimensions, and finalization status. `anchors.json` must either become that defined artifact or be removed from the architecture diagram.
- A display-reconfiguration event payload sufficient to replay pointer placement: old/new display topology, coordinate transforms, scale, capture rect, and effective timestamp.
- Defined visual/audio semantics for track absence, stream rebuild gaps, frame drops, and device loss.
- Preview architecture. The document says Electron owns “compositing, export via WebCodecs,” but does not say whether preview invokes the same replay/transform module as export or whether it has a separate live code path. The latter is the bug class being avoided.
- A bounded first milestone that proves the invariant: one display track, synthetic event fixture, fixed-step replay, and byte/stable-decision comparison between preview and export. Camera, mic, system audio, hot-swap, and device loss can follow that proof.

## Overconfidence

- **“VFR at capture, CFR at export” is treated as a settled implementation choice, but it does not settle deterministic temporal sampling.** The difficult part is not choosing VFR/CFR; it is defining how arbitrary VFR source PTS become fixed-step simulation inputs.
- **“CGEvent.timestamp used as-is” assumes a timestamp’s epoch is enough.** Shared epoch does not specify ordering at equal timestamps, event-to-frame sampling semantics, coordinate-space validity after display changes, or persistence precision across JSON serialization.
- **“same stream, separate output type; avoids a second device path” understates system-audio integration risk.** It may avoid a separate hardware device, but it does not define system-audio timing, interruption behavior, segment alignment, or the export policy for audio discontinuities.
- **“one grant, against the signed bundle the user recognises” is presented more strongly than the document’s own evidence supports.** The helper’s TCC attribution under Electron spawning, signing identity, bundle placement, and rebuild behavior remains unmeasured. The document correctly proposes a probe later, but should not describe the model as established before that probe passes.
- **“The slice builds each [risk] in as a first-class concern” is not supported by the increment ordering.** The slice schedules the mechanisms, but not the persisted contracts and replay tests needed to make their failure modes deterministic.
- **“Composite + export” is described as porting a spike harness.** A harness that exports one result is not evidence that preview and export share a transform. Porting it without first extracting a pure replay contract is likely to fossilize its incidental timing assumptions.

## Verdict

**Revise it.** The plan is credible as a capture-infrastructure plan, but it does not yet enforce the one invariant it identifies as non-negotiable: the first irreversible divergence enters when increment 2 persists separately timed tracks and events without a canonical, immutable replay timeline, and the current increment boundaries will hide that until phase 4 makes WebCodecs and ad hoc alignment logic the de facto clock. **Before any live capture porting, define and implement a versioned finalized-session/project manifest plus a shared fixed-step `transform(project, events, tick)` module, then prove preview and export replay the same fixture with identical per-tick decisions.**

## Reviewer D

## Issues

**The transform purity is not a property of this document at all — it is absent from it.** Phase 1 defines the capture path (helper) and the export path (increment 4: "WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness"), but nowhere does it specify *where* the transform lives, what its inputs are, or what boundary separates evaluation from rendering/export. The stated non-negotiable — `transform(project, events, t)` evaluated on a fixed simulation step — appears nowhere in the architecture diagram, the risk table, the increments, or the non-goals. The first concrete place a violation enters is **increment 2, the moment events are written to `events.json` as a side effect of the event tap**: there is no defined event model, no stated `t` domain (mach ticks? `displayTimeNs`? CFR timestamps? all three appear in the settled table with no reconciliation), and no rule that says the composite step must re-evaluate deterministically from project + events rather than snapshot whatever the helper happened to emit. A violation would be invisible: the phase boundaries (helper → JSON lines → Electron) are transport boundaries, not evaluation boundaries. They would hide a stateful transform inside the Electron WebCodecs pipeline exactly as effectively as they'd hide one in the helper. Nothing in the structure as written would catch it, because nothing is asserted.

**The document claims phase 0 "proved each component in isolation" but catalogues known failures.** "Thermal fade was already visible: 18.7 → 12.1 fps across a longer benchmark" is a component that does not work under sustained load, not a component proven in isolation. The spike proved the happy path; the point of phase 1 is to discover exactly the failure modes already known to exist. This is fine as a goal, but "settled by phase 0" frames it as verified behaviour. The 30-minute soak will produce data, not proof.

**The signing plan depends on a machine state that is asserted as trivially fixable but may not be.** "Both keychains are empty, `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` reports 0" — and then the document describes making a cert via Keychain Access. If the author has never done this on this machine, there is no reason to believe it is the first thing that works on the first try; Keychain Access certificate creation on a headless-ish dev box, with no Xcode, has its own known failure modes. The verification step ("build, grant, rebuild, re-run probe") is good, but the document does not say what happens if the probe fails: fall back to ad-hoc with a warning, but ad-hoc revokes TCC on every rebuild — which means the entire dev loop of increments 1–5 depends on a certificate path that has never been executed on this machine and for which all fallbacks are already known-bad. The slice's first milestone (increment 1, "no capture") actually doesn't need TCC; but the first moment capture is ported in (increment 2), the build is blocked if the cert route failed.

## Missing

**The event-to-transform contract.** Nowhere is there a definition of what constitutes "the event," what fields `events.json` contains, what clock domain each field is in, or how events relate to captured frames at composite time. `displayTimeNs = displayTime × 125/3` is a clock conversion on display timestamps; the event tap has its own timestamp domain; camera/mic PTS are mach ticks. The table settles each independently; nothing settles what happens when you align them. The first concrete rewrite will be forced by this: when increment 4 starts compositing cursor over frame, the author will discover events cannot be aligned to frames without a model of which clock is master and what resampling is legal. That is not a bug to be found in the soak; it is a data model that does not exist.

**Where the transform function lives and where its outputs are cached.** Is the simulation step in the helper, writing a timeline to disk, or in Electron, reading `events.json` and evaluating per export? The answer determines whether the "pure function" rule even has a host. If it lives in Electron, then the helper's `events.json` is the single source of truth and the helper must be written so events are append-only and time-ordered. If it lives in the helper, then Electron is a dumb consumer and the whole WebCodecs pipeline is just a renderer of a pre-evaluated timeline — but then the helper needs the whole project model, which contradicts the architecture diagram, where the helper only knows capture.

**Exit/restart semantics for the helper.** "Long-running, controllable process" with no defined behaviour on crash, on JSON protocol desync, on Electron death leaving an orphan helper, or on helper upgrade while a session is live. A long-running Swift process has different failure modes than a one-shot; the document says "controllable" but does not say what control looks like beyond spawn/supervise. Increment 3 will force these decisions; they should be made before increment 1 ships.

**What is in a session dir after a crash.** Segmenting output every N minutes is a stability mechanic. But the document does not state whether a half-written segment is recoverable, whether `events.json` is flushed per segment or once per session, or what the recovery rule is when the helper dies mid-segment. The soak harness will find the first instance of this; it's already known to be a real risk, and the phase should decide it before the soak rather than treating the soak as the place where decisions surface.

**Audio capture qualifiers.** `SCStreamConfiguration.capturesAudio` is listed as risk mitigation, but the document does not address whether this captures *system* audio or *microphone* audio — the two are separate concerns on macOS. The settled table separately lists `mic.wav` and `sysaudio.wav` as outputs; the risk table mentions only "system audio." If the slice is actually recording both via `capturesAudio` and separate AVCaptureSession, that's two audio paths and the architecture diagram doesn't show the second. If the slice is recording only system audio and not mic, the table claims mic.wav is in scope, and the slice's first end-to-end export has no camera PiP audio. The document says "camera/mic are already off the critical path" — but their outputs are still on the composite path when increment 4 runs, and nothing says how.

## Overconfidence

**"Camera/mic are already off the critical path."** Not for phase 1 they're not. Increment 4 requires camera PiP in the composite. If the camera device disappears, the composite must still export; the document says the helper handles device loss with a warning, but does not say what the resulting `session dir` contains for the composite to read. A missing camera track that was assumed present is a critical-path defect in increment 4, discovered long after the helper was written to "keep recording" without saying what it keeps recording *to*.

**"The spike proved these composed"** appears once, but the spike did not compose them — the document says so explicitly: phase 1 "proves them composed." Phase 0 proved components in isolation. The conflation of "component works in isolation" with "component is ready for composition" is the exact overconfidence phase 1 is designed to punish. The document should name which components were only isolation-tested and treat the composition as unproven, or state what the spike's "harness" (referenced in increment 4) actually ran. "WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness" implies the spike did already compose, which contradicts the stated goal.

**The 30-minute soak as coverage for all four risks.** A single soak run exercises all four failure modes simultaneously — which is good for the system, but bad for knowing *which* failure broke what. If the soak finds a dropped frame, the author will not know whether it was the display change, the device unplug, the system audio capture, or the thermal throttle. The table explicitly lists "long recording + display change + device unplug + system audio" as one harness; that conflates independent risk isolation. Phase 1 should at minimum run each induced failure separately before running them all at once. As written, the soak produces a single data point about a four-variable interaction, which is close to unactionable.

**"Only the first [signing need] is blocking" — but the document's own stable-identity verification step has never been run.** The entire paragraph asserts a self-signed cert "gives a stable identity so TCC grants survive rebuilds," then immediately says "it has not been measured on this machine yet." On this machine, with empty keychains, no Xcode, and no prior art, the claim is a hypothesis. The phase plan hangs two increments (2 and 3) on it without a fallback beyond ad-hoc, which is known-bad. The document is honest about the uncertainty, which is good; what's overconfident is calling the self-signed route "not blocking" while simultaneously saying it's unverified and there is no fallback. Until the probe passes, the dev loop is blocked.

## Verdict

Revise it. The phase plan is honest about its own unknowns and its component structure is sensible — but the document entirely fails to name the transform boundary, which is the single most important architectural decision for phase 1, and without it the "pure function of project + events + t" is a slogan, not a structure. The increments will collapse at 4, when composite time arrives and the event model is discovered to not exist; the soak will not save it because the soak tests runtime stability, not correctness.

The single highest-leverage change: **write the transform contract before increment 2.** Define, concretely: what `events.json` contains per event (type, timestamp domain, payload), which clock is master, what the simulation step length and phase are, where the transform lives (helper or Electron), and what the composite consumes. ## Reviewer E

## Issues

**1. The purity invariant appears nowhere in this document, so the plan asserts nothing — it just omits it.** Search PHASE-1.md for "pure", "deterministic", "simulation step", "transform", "project", "preview": none present. The only line touching the render path is increment 4: *"Composite + export — WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness: one CFR MP4 with cursor overlay and camera PiP."* That is a description of an output artifact, not of a function. The module structure shown (Electron does "UI, compositing, export"; Swift does capture) draws its boundary in the wrong place for this invariant: it separates *capture from render*, when the boundary that matters is *transform from sink*. The rule you need on the diagram is:

> `render(project, events, t) → FrameState` is a pure function with no persistent state and no access to wall-clock, decoder scheduling, or "current" anything. Preview and export are two **sinks** that call it with different `t` sequences. There is exactly one call site chain; a sink may never fork the transform.

Nothing in this document expresses or enforces that. So: **it only asserts it, and here not even that.**

**2. The first concrete violation is already baked into a "settled" decision: VFR at capture, CFR at export, with no stated resampling rule.** `display.mp4` has frames at arbitrary `displayTimeNs`, and phase 0 measured throttling to 12.1 fps — so under load a single source frame can span five output frames. Export must answer "which source frame is visible at t = n/fps?" Preview, if written the way preview always gets written, answers a *different* question: `requestVideoFrameCallback` / `video.currentTime` / "latest decoded frame", i.e. whatever the decoder happened to hand over on this display refresh. Those two sampling rules disagree by exactly one frame most of the time and by five frames during a thermal dip. That is `export ≠ preview`, entering at the first line of increment 4, from a decision the doc marks *do not relitigate*. The missing rule:

> Video is an **input sampled by t**, not a stream that plays. At time `t`, the visible source frame is the one with the greatest PTS ≤ `t`. Hold, never interpolate, never "latest available". Same code, both sinks.

**3. The second violation, and the more likely one given your instincts: per-frame accumulator smoothing.** "Cursor overlay" and (inevitably) click ripples, zoom-follow-cursor, auto-camera. The natural way to write cursor easing is `pos += (target - pos) * k` once per rendered frame. That makes the result a function of *how many times you were called*, which differs between a 60 Hz preview that drops frames under load and an export loop that never drops one. It will look correct in both and differ in both. The fixed simulation step is the fix, but the document never states the step, never says the accumulator lives in the sink, and never says that state must be reconstructible from `t` alone. Concretely: `stepSim(state, dt=1/120)` from `t=0`, sinks own the accumulator, transform exposes `stateAt(n)` where `n = floor(t * 120)`, and export samples exact integer `n` — no float seconds anywhere, all times integer ns or integer sim ticks.

**4. Camera PiP is the third: two independent decoders.** Compositing camera from a second `HTMLVideoElement` couples the frame you get to decoder scheduling and to `playbackRate` drift. Export will pull ahead or behind by tens of milliseconds depending on machine load. Same rule as (2), applied per track, sampled by PTS bracket against the single canonical timeline.

**5. Phase boundaries hide all of this rather than catching it.** Increment 4 is the only increment that touches the transform, and it delivers preview and export *in the same step* with no differential gate. Increment 5 soaks the *capture* side (display change, unplug, sysaudio) and says nothing about render determinism. There is no increment whose exit criterion is: *the same session exported twice is byte-identical*, or *the hash of preview frame at t equals the hash of export frame at t for 200 sampled t across the recording*. Without that gate, a violation entering at increment 4 ships. Worse, increment 4 is *last*, so the on-disk contract (`events.json`, `anchors.json`, segment layout) is frozen by increments 1–2 before the only consumer that knows what it needs exists.

**6. Phase 1 as scoped may contain no preview path at all**, in which case the invariant the whole architecture exists to protect is untestable in this phase. Increment 4 says "export". Non-goals exclude "editing UI" but say nothing about preview. Decide explicitly: either increment 4 ships both sinks over one transform (and the differential test), or state plainly that `export ≠ preview` is unverified until phase 2 — and accept that increments 1–3 are freezing a format against an untested consumer.

**7. Stats over stdout can stall capture.** "Emit periodic `stats`" on the same pipe as control JSON, consumed by an Electron main process that in increment 4 is also driving a WebCodecs encode loop. Node's stdio pipe buffer is finite (~64 KB); if the reader doesn't drain, the helper's `write()` blocks. If that write happens on the capture or encode queue, you drop real frames because the UI was busy. Rule: control is reliable and back-pressured; telemetry is lossy and non-blocking (drop on full, coalesce to latest). Never let a diagnostic channel apply back-pressure to the capture graph.

**8. Increment 1 is four subsystems, not one sitting.** Long-running process + JSON-line protocol (with framing, versioning, request/response correlation, error frames) + display reconfiguration watcher + device watchers. Split: (1a) process lifecycle + protocol echo + clean shutdown; (1b) watchers as protocol events. "In progress" on a four-subsystem increment is how a phase loses its shape.

**9. Signing/TCC: the rebuild test as described tests the wrong rebuild.** If the helper ships inside the Electron bundle, replacing the helper binary breaks the bundle's seal and invalidates the *parent's* signature — which is the identity TCC granted. Your verification needs two distinct experiments: (a) rebuild the helper only, re-sign the helper, re-sign the bundle, re-probe; (b) rebuild the whole bundle. They can have different answers. Also: a self-signed cert only appears in `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` once its trust settings allow code signing — the Certificate Assistant default will bite you here.

**10. Multi-display hot-swap breaks the transform's coordinate space, and the doc treats it as a capture-side concern only.** `CGEvent` cursor positions are in global display coordinates; capture is per-display; a reconfiguration mid-recording changes the mapping. The transform at time `t` must resolve cursor coordinates through the geometry that was active *at that event's timestamp*. Rule: **anything the world does mid-recording is a timestamped event in the timeline; nothing about the world is a scalar field on the project.** Display geometry is a versioned timeline in `events.json`, not `project.width/height`. If you load geometry as project metadata, everything after the first hot-swap is wrong in both sinks — which the differential test will *not* catch, because both are wrong identically.

## Missing

- **The canonical timeline, stated as a rule.** One clock (mach host ns), zero at recording start, every artifact converted at ingest and never at render. Phase 0 settled the *conversions*; nobody has said where they're applied. If any conversion happens inside the transform, the transform depends on capture-time facts and is no longer a function of `project + events + t`.
- **Schemas for `events.json` and `anchors.json`.** These two files are the load-bearing surface of the purity claim and they exist here as filenames. What is an anchor? Presumably segment-PTS ↔ host-clock, but unstated. Write both schemas plus a hand-authored fixture session *before* increment 1, and develop the transform against the fixture. That inverts the phase order in the one place it needs inverting.
- **The simulation step, as a number**, and the rule when export fps is not a multiple of it.
- **Segment boundary semantics.** Segmenting every N minutes creates joins. Gap or overlap? Does the transform see one continuous timeline (resolved at ingest) or a segment list (resolved at render)? The former is the only answer compatible with purity.
- **Missing-frame policy** during thermal throttle (12.1 fps → CFR 60): hold-last is the only deterministic option; say it once, in one place.
- **Audio timeline rule.** +1.8 ms camera↔mic is a constant to apply where? If preview mixes via `AudioContext` with live drift and export mixes sample-accurately, A/V sync differs between paths — the same bug class, in the channel nobody tests.
- **Per-increment acceptance criteria.** "Ugly is fine" with no exit condition on any of the five increments means no increment can be declared done.
- **Helper crash policy mid-recording.** Is a session with a truncated final segment recoverable and exportable? This determines whether `anchors.json` is written incrementally or at close.
- **The one-renderer rule, written down.** The obvious future pressure is "WebCodecs export is too slow, do it natively in the helper." That creates a second implementation and permanently guarantees `export ≠ preview`. Pre-commit: exactly one renderer; if export is slow, it runs slower.

## Overconfidence

- "Inherits Electron's TCC identity — one grant" is stated as a mechanism, not a measurement. Responsible-process attribution for a spawned helper depends on how it's launched and where it lives in the bundle; ScreenCaptureKit is one of the pickier consumers. This deserves the same treatment you gave the clock offsets: probe it, record the number.
- The signing section is admirably honest ("has not been measured"), and then increment 1 proceeds as if it were settled. If the self-signed cert doesn't produce a stable designated requirement, your dev loop re-grants Screen Recording on every rebuild — which changes how you work, not just what you sign. Test it before increment 1, not during.
- "Composite + export — WebCodecs pipeline from the spike's harness" reads as *port existing code*. It isn't: the spike's harness almost certainly renders from playing media elements at display rate. Rewriting it as a sampled, seekable, stateless transform is the hardest engineering in phase 1 and is presented as the smallest increment. Its state handling (WebCodecs `VideoDecoder` seek, keyframe scan, output-ordering, exact PTS bracketing across segments) is real TypeScript with real ordering invariants — the part of the stack you've said is slowest for you, scheduled last, with no fixture to develop against.
- "Three [risks] are the same underlying problem" is true of the capture side and false of the transform side: hot-swap and device loss both mutate the *timeline*, and that consequence is nowhere in the plan.
- The thermal number (18.7 → 12.1 fps) sits in the risk table as an observability story, but it silently invalidates the unset target capture resolution/fps for phase 1. There is no stated capture target to soak against.

## Verdict

**Revise** — the capture half of this plan is strong, measured, and correctly sequenced; the render half is a single sentence in increment 4 and it is where your stated non-negotiable dies. The phase boundaries as drawn would *hide* the first violation, not catch it: VFR→CFR resampling and per-frame easing both enter inside one increment that produces preview and export together with no differential gate, arriving after the on-disk contract is frozen by increments that never had to consume it. That's a rewrite discovered at the end of phase 1, on the code you're slowest at, against a format you can no longer change cheaply.

**Highest-leverage change:** insert increment 0 — write the `events.json` / `anchors.json` schemas and a hand-authored 5-second fixture session, then build `render(project, events, t)` against the fixture with two sinks (canvas preview, WebCodecs encode) and one exit test: *for 200 sampled t, the preview frame hash equals the export frame hash, and two exports of the fixture are byte-identical.* Do that before the helper writes its first real session, and the transform's purity becomes a gate every later increment must pass through instead of a sentence in an architecture doc.
